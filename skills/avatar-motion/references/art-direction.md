# The Avatar Motion illustration style

Open `style-reference.png` in this directory. It is the visual authority for this skill, not merely an optional inspiration image. The picture shows the intended finish; the pictured person is an example, not a face template.

## Keep the visual language

- **Silhouette:** friendly rounded cheek/jaw/chin construction with modest shape exaggeration. Clean intentional curves, not a polygonal mannequin or a circle with features pasted on.
- **Eyes:** generously sized ivory whites and dark oval pupils, a confident dark upper lash, little internal detail. Account for perspective: the farther eye and ear may appear smaller. Avoid glassy 3D eyeballs or realistic irises.
- **Nose and cheeks:** compact rounded coral/red nose, slightly darker underside and small soft highlight; gentle blush shapes. Relate warmth to the person's natural skin tone rather than lightening their skin to match the sample.
- **Ears:** slightly enlarged, rounded and clearly constructed, with one simple warm inner fold. They should feel integrated with the face, not oversized comic props.
- **Hair:** recognisable hairstyle described by a few sculpted curved clumps, overlapping dark shapes and controlled lighter planes. Enough detail to match the reference, but no strand-by-strand rendering or polygon facets. Preserve curls, texture, hairline and colour appropriate to the person. Only identifiable free tips get motion.
- **Brows and mouth:** one dark shade per brow; a readable expressive smile with a simple mouth cavity, restrained teeth/tongue shapes when visible. Avoid realistic individual teeth.
- **Finish:** polished 2D Illustrator-like illustration with crisp edges, broad warm colour regions, controlled cel shading and gentle gradients. No photographic skin texture, noisy grain, paper effects, heavy black outline around everything, 3D gloss or excessive vector simplification.
- **Bust:** head remains dominant; connected neck, believable collar and shoulder silhouette. A horizontal lower shirt cut suits a navigation slot. Neither the red checked shirt nor blue backdrop is mandatory.

## Let the person vary

Match their face proportions, recognisable nose/jaw characteristics, hairline and hairstyle, skin tone, facial hair, clothing and gender presentation. Use the reference's modest exaggeration and rendering treatment on these features. Do not clone the example's head or turn every subject into the same young boy. The head may face front or a slight three-quarter angle; both eyes need to remain visible for the bundled renderer.

## Generation brief

Use the person's photo as **identity reference** and `style-reference.png` as a separate **style reference**, using the image inputs supported by the available tool. Adapt this brief to that tool:

> Make one illustrated head-and-shoulders avatar of the person in the identity photo. Preserve their recognisable face, natural skin tone, hairstyle and hair texture. Use the same 2D cartoon rendering language and finish as the style-reference image: clean rounded silhouettes, large ivory oval eyes with dark oval pupils, confident upper lashes, single-colour eyebrows, subtly enlarged ears, a small warm coral-red nose with a soft highlight, gentle rosy cheeks, sculpted hair clumps and restrained warm shaded planes. Carry this style onto this person's features rather than copying the example person's face, hair, red shirt or background. Keep the bust's neck and collar anatomically connected. Both eyes open and visible, friendly relaxed expression, uncluttered composition, no text. Match the reference's level of illustration detail; no generic flat icon, geometric low-poly substitute, anime, 3D, photographic or paper-textured finish. For navigation use, prefer a transparent background, headroom and a straight lower torso baseline.

Do not pre-fit coordinates before seeing the actual generated image. Inspect the finished art against both references before adding motion. A small coral nose alone does not establish style fidelity if the eyes, ears, hair and shading have drifted. If the generation is insufficient, revise the specific mismatch and inspect again. If further iterations cannot reach the reference quality, explain the limitation and let the user choose before proceeding.

The skill provides references and a workflow, not a trained style model. Different image tools can produce different results; reference use and visual review are required, and exact reproducibility is not guaranteed.
